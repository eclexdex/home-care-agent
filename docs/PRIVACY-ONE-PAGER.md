# A note about the system in your home

To you — and to the family who set this up with you:

This page explains, in plain words, what the little devices in your home do,
what they never do, and how you stay in charge of them. Nobody has to love
technology for this to be worth having. You only have to agree to it — and
you can change your mind.

## What the system notices

- **Buttons you press.** The button by your chair is yours: one press means
  "I need help now," and your family's phones go off right away. The button
  by the door just tells the house whether you're home or out — you flip it,
  nobody else does.
- **Doors opening.** The house only pays attention to doors when *you* have
  said you're away. While you're home, the doors are news to no one.
- **Movement in shared rooms at night.** This one exists for a simple
  reason: when you get up at 3 a.m., a soft, low light comes on along your
  path to the kitchen, so you don't trip in the dark. That's the whole job.
- **Temperature.** The house notices if it gets dangerously cold, or if the
  heat fails in winter — because a cold house is dangerous, for pipes and
  for people.
- **Water leaks,** if your family added the leak sensors. A small alert now
  beats a ruined floor later.

Every one of these is there to help you stay in your own home. None of them
is there to watch you.

## What the system never notices

- **No cameras inside.** Not one. If your family ever puts a camera up, it
  stays outside the house, and that is your family's choice to discuss with
  you — never a surprise.
- **No microphones.** Nothing listens to conversations. Nothing ever will.
- **No wearables.** Nothing to wear, nothing to charge, nothing that scolds
  you for forgetting it.
- **No guesses about your health.** The system doesn't score you, diagnose
  you, or predict anything about you. It reacts only to the specific things
  your family set up, on this page.
- **Nothing leaves your house** except an alert, to people you chose, over a
  channel your family controls. No company collects it. No one sells it. No
  one analyzes it.

## Who can see the alerts

Only the people on your **care team** — the list of names you helped choose
on install day (the current list lives here: CARE_TEAM_NAMES, updated by your
family when it changes). If a name on that list stops feeling right, say so
and it comes off. Being on the list means you have a reason to get an alert,
not just curiosity.

## How to pause anything

- **The button by the door** controls the watchful parts. Press it and the
  door watch goes deaf; press it again and it's armed. You own it. Nobody
  arms or disarms the house over your head.
- **The master switch** — the one your family agreed with you about — stops
  the alerts when you want them stopped.
- **Unplugging is always allowed.** If a device bothers you, unplugging it
  is not causing trouble. It's your home.

## Your consent doesn't expire

You can say "this part bothers me" at any time — on install day, six months
from now, or over breakfast next Tuesday. When you do, the system changes.
No arguing, no guilt, no lecture. If a part can't be changed to suit you,
that part goes. Dignity is the point; safety is supposed to serve it, not
the other way around.
